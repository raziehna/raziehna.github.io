Research

My research lies at the intersection of statistics, machine learning, and public health. I develop methods for causal inference from incomplete, biased, high-dimensional, and otherwise imperfect data, with the goal of improving the reliability of scientific evidence and supporting informed decision making. For a publication list, visit Google Scholar.

Research philosophy

Understanding what can be learned from imperfect data, and when the data alone are not enough.

Research themes Grant support Research code

Research themes

The themes below separate methodological questions by the type of imperfection or structure in the data.

02

(Un)measured confounding

Graphical identification, semiparametric estimation, and sensitivity analysis when important causes are latent or unobserved.

 01

Missing data

Identification and estimation of causal and statisical parameters when observations are coarsened, or selectively observed and missing not at random.

 05

Constrained learning, algorithmic fairness

Statistical learning and decision-making methods that satisfy fairness, policy, or other causal and statistical constraints.

 04

High-dimensional data

Causal dimension reduction methods for multi/high-dimensional confounders, exposures, or mediators.

 03

Mediation analysis

Methods for inferring mediation and path-specific effects in complex longitudinal and multi-mediator settings.

 06

Health and public health data science

Applications of causal and statistical methods to public health, medicine, and biomedical science.
